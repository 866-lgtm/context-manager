- Transient provider failures no longer spend the L1 compression ladder or exhaust a request family. A failure is evidence about the request only when it is a deterministic rejection (`context_length`, `invalid_request`, `safety`, `unsupported`). Capacity, transport, credential and unclassified failures used to be recorded as a rung's `provider_error` while the ladder carried on, so an outage could quarantine a viable family. Now such a failure stops the ladder at that rung and keeps what the ladder had learned, and a later tick resumes there.
  - Durable family progress (branch-scoped state `autobio:compression-family-progress`, separate from the quarantine ledger): every deterministic answer a request has given is written and synced before the next rung's call, so neither a restart nor a resumed tick pays again for the canonical or for a rung whose outcome is known. It is one record shared by every strategy on the store and namespace: each read is fresh and each write is a read-merge-write of its own family, so a sibling strategy sees another's progress and no write drops another's families. It is dropped when the chunk mints or its span is covered otherwise (an existing L1 adopted, a sibling's coalesced result, or the dedup guard finding every message already summarized), when the family exhausts into quarantine, and when an operator clears that quarantine. A sweep from the maintenance tick, every 10 minutes at most and independent of the quarantine alarm, also drops progress whose chunk record is compressed, progress whose chunk record is missing when every message of its span is covered by an L1 or one is gone from the store, and progress nothing has advanced for 30 days. A ladder never writes back a family dropped while it ran.
  - The plain-prose retry (after an answer whose whole text was a `<thinking>` wrapper) is a rung of the same ladder: the empty answer is recorded against the rung that gave it (the canonical, or its no-tools or tools-less retry), a rejection of the retry is its outcome, and a transient failure resumes at the retry.
  - The degraded text-only retry after a transport rejects a request's reasoning carriers is entered only for a failure classified as a request-attributable rejection (a transient failure that merely mentions thinking interrupts the ladder instead), and the rejection is recorded under its own identity, so a resumed ladder goes straight to the stripped request instead of sending the rejected one again. Merges apply the same classification before their degraded retry.
  - A request-attributable rejection of the canonical (including its no-tools or tools-less retries) skips the recall variants and the canonical hoist. Each variant reuses the rejected request and only expands recall. The rejection goes to source-only-final once, when configured, and otherwise exhausts the family.
  - A failure that is not a rejection says nothing about the request by its kind, but it is evidence by the company it keeps. It counts against the request only when some other compression has landed since the request's last failure (an L1 pushed, a merged summary written; a stale merge entry skipped with no call is no landing), and only as a repeat of the same failure class (membrane's error type and HTTP status; another class starts a new run). The 8th such failure of one request decides its rung, like a rejection: the ladder tries source-only-final once, when configured, and otherwise the family is exhausted and quarantined like any other. It stays quarantined until its chunk compresses, the paid-off sweep clears it, or an operator does. So an outage, in which nothing lands, never exhausts anything, however long it lasts, while a request that keeps failing the same way as other work lands is decided. At a 30% failure rate, eight in a row is about 0.007% per request. For operators: the run is kept in the family's durable progress, keyed by the hash of the request actually sent (the stripped request after a carrier rejection), and the lane's evidence in its own state (`autobio:compression-lane-evidence`), advanced only after a success's result is written; both are synced when written, so they survive a restart or a crash. Split-stitch is one atomic retry unit: a transient failure inside it re-runs the whole rung later, within its existing call caps.
- Compression is paced across ticks after a transient failure, in L1 or a merge. No compression call is made and no further queue item is consumed until both the provider's stated `retryAfterMs` and a local backoff of 30 s doubling to 10 minutes have passed. The chunk a failing tick was trying goes to the back of the queue, so the chunks behind it run once the pause ends, and a failing merge goes behind the other queued merges (a chunk is found by its source identity among the current branch's chunks, only if it is still uncompressed, and only if no rebuild re-decided the queue while the call was in flight, so work a newer eligibility decision withdrew stays withdrawn). After a deferral by the caller's provider admission or an abort, which say nothing, the chunk keeps its place at the head. A merge spanning a chunk that was put back waits for it: a run of summaries before a hole that a retry will fill isn't consolidated early as a stranded interior run.
  - A request that fails while nothing else lands has nothing to compare against, so it is never counted and backs off indefinitely. After an hour of failures with nothing landing, the lane alerts once per stall: a warning, a `compression:lane-stalled` log event, and a structured `compressionStall` in `checkReadiness()` (since when, what keeps failing, its failure class) until a compression lands. If the provider is answering, the request may be failing on its own: the stall lasts until other work lands (its failures then count) or someone fixes the cause.
  - A stated wait is a non-negative `retryAfterMs` (NaN, or any other value, states none) and is never shortened. One that cannot be held as an instant (Infinity, or past the last instant a Date can hold) is an explicit hold until a restart, a model change, or a release.
  - Any provider answer resets the pause, a merge's included (a refusal is an answer).
  - The pause is in process and scoped to the membrane instance and to the model the failed call was dispatched with: another model ignores and clears it. A restart-stable provider deadline belongs to the caller's provider admission. Discarded or stale-branch work installs nothing.
  - `releaseCompressionPause(model?)` releases the provider's stated wait (agent-framework calls it when an operator releases a provider wait). The lane's own backoff stays, so the lane resumes once that has passed too, and the failure count stays (a release is not an answer from the provider).
  - A refusal by the caller's provider admission (`providerAdmission: 'deferred'`: agent-framework holding a model, no call made) counts no failure and adds no backoff; any genuine backoff already in force is left as it was. The admission stays the authority: its answer is cached until the deadline it gave or for at most 30 s (`COMPRESSION_ADMISSION_RECHECK_MS`), whichever is sooner, and the lane then asks it again. Asking makes no provider call. The cached answer expires within 30 s, and the next otherwise-eligible tick asks the admission again (a genuine backoff, or the maintenance cadence, can postpone that tick), so a condition that clears with no release reaching this instance (a recovered journal, a release made in another process) is observed without one; `releaseCompressionPause` still resumes at once. A deferral never shortens a wait a provider stated after a real call.
  - One warning per pause, a `compression:lane-paused` log event, and `checkReadiness()` names the pause while it lasts. A hold by the caller's provider admission warns, and logs `compression:lane-held-by-admission`, once per hold and again when its deadline changes, though the lane asks the admission again every 30 s at most.
- The merge lane routes only request-attributable rejections through its bounded attempt policy. A non-retryable failure that says nothing about the request (an expired key, a spent quota) now pauses the lane instead of spending attempts toward quarantine. Any other failure counts under the same evidence rule as L1's: the 8th failure of one class, each after other compression landed, is a bounded-policy attempt, which pauses nothing (as in L1, the deciding failure is the request's own), so the policy's smaller retry is asked at the next tick. A queued merge that is skipped with no call (a source missing or already parented, or a crossed merge refused) is dropped from the queue as before, but it is neither an answer that ends the pause nor a landing. For subclasses: the protected `executeMerge` now resolves `true` when it wrote the merged parent and `false` when it skipped, and an override must resolve `true` for a merge it wrote. The persisted merge entry carries the run in place of its server-error streak, so the merge lane no longer counts 12 consecutive server errors in an outage.
- Compression failure logs (`Failed to compress chunk`, `Failed to merge summaries`, the speculative-drain error) print a bounded string instead of the error object, and the L1 and merge `CONTEXT_MANAGER_COMPRESSION_LOG` records bound their `error` field to 2,000 characters. A provider error's `rawRequest` is the whole request that failed, and printing the object wrote it out in full.
