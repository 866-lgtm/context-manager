- Transient provider failures no longer spend the L1 compression ladder or exhaust a request family by their kind. By its kind alone, a failure is evidence about the request only when it is a deterministic rejection (`context_length`, `invalid_request`, `safety`, `unsupported`); any other failure is evidence only by the company it keeps, below. Capacity, transport, credential and unclassified failures used to be recorded as a rung's `provider_error` while the ladder carried on, so an outage could quarantine a viable family. Now such a failure stops the ladder at that rung and keeps what the ladder had learned, and a later tick resumes there.
  - Durable family progress (branch-scoped state `autobio:compression-family-progress`, separate from the quarantine ledger): every deterministic answer a request has given is written and synced before the next rung's call, so neither a restart nor a resumed tick pays again for the canonical or for a rung whose outcome is known. It is one record shared by every strategy on the store and namespace: each read is fresh and each write is a read-merge-write of its own family, so a sibling strategy sees another's progress and no write drops another's families. It is dropped when the chunk mints or its span is covered otherwise (an existing L1 adopted, a sibling's coalesced result, or the dedup guard finding every message already summarized), when the family exhausts into quarantine, and when an operator clears that quarantine. A sweep from the maintenance tick, every 10 minutes at most and independent of the quarantine alarm, also drops progress whose chunk record is compressed, progress whose chunk record is missing when every message of its span is covered by an L1 or one is gone from the store, and progress nothing has advanced for 30 days (with no chunk records at all, only age decides). A ladder never writes back a family dropped while it ran. A recorded deterministic answer belongs to its request (its bytes, the model included) and is reused on any route and in any later process, which is what keeps it paid once. After a known route change, an answer that only the old backend would give (an `unsupported` from a missing feature, a stricter `safety` filter, a proxy's size limit read as `invalid_request`) still stands for its family, and the family can exhaust where the new route would have answered its canonical; `clearCompressionRefusalQuarantine()` asks it again from its canonical, with its recorded answers dropped. A family whose ladder is still in progress keeps its answers, and may mint from a lesser rung than the new route would have answered.
  - The plain-prose retry (after an answer whose whole text was a `<thinking>` wrapper) is a rung of the same ladder: the empty answer is recorded against the rung that gave it (the canonical, or its no-tools or tools-less retry), a rejection of the retry is its outcome, and a transient failure resumes at the retry.
  - The degraded text-only retry after a transport rejects a request's reasoning carriers is entered only for a failure classified as a request-attributable rejection (a transient failure that merely mentions thinking interrupts the ladder instead), and the rejection is recorded under its own identity, so a resumed ladder goes straight to the stripped request instead of sending the rejected one again. Merges apply the same classification before their degraded retry.
  - A request-attributable rejection of the canonical (including its no-tools or tools-less retries) skips the recall variants and the canonical hoist. Each variant reuses the rejected request and only expands recall. The rejection goes to source-only-final once, when configured, and otherwise exhausts the family.
  - A failure that is not a rejection says nothing about the request by its kind, but it is evidence by the company it keeps on its own route. A subject's first failure starts a run, and each later one counts against it (an L1 chunk's rung, or a merge) only when some other compression has landed in the same domain since the subject's last failure there: the membrane instance the call went through and the model it named, as this process knows them. Another model's or another route's landings say nothing about this one. A landing is a compression the provider answered and whose result is written (an L1 pushed and its chunk record marked, or a merged summary written); a thin-chunk stub, an adopted L1, a span found covered and a merge entry skipped with no call are none. Failures of any class count. An L1 run is keyed by the chunk's rung, not by the bytes it sent, because every L1 that lands changes a pending chunk's request (its recall frontier grows), so the run goes on across that. The rung is named by the chunk's sources, their ids and their content, and by its label: a source edited in place starts a new run, and a late failure of the old content leaves the new content's run alone. Each content of a chunk has one run, held by one of its request families: a write that carries it moves it there, a family that holds none takes it, and an answer or a decision ends it in all of them. The 8th failure in a run, the first and seven more each after a landing, decides the rung like a rejection: the ladder tries source-only-final once, when configured, and otherwise the family is exhausted and quarantined like any other. It stays quarantined until its chunk compresses, the paid-off sweep clears it, or an operator does. So an outage, in which nothing lands, never exhausts anything, however long it lasts, while a subject that keeps failing as other work lands on its route is decided. If each call failed independently 30% of the time, a given eight attempts would all fail 0.007% of the time (0.3⁸); a decision also needs a landing between each pair, so it is rarer still. For operators: the count is this process's evidence. The run itself is stored, and synced, with its subject (the family's progress; a merge's queue entry), so after a restart it still holds merges around its span, and its next failure starts a new count: nothing durable can say the new process's route is the old one. So a process that restarts before it collects eight counts keeps retrying a request that fails on its own while other work lands, paced, with no decision and, since things land, no stall alert. Routing below membrane (a proxy that picks a backend per call) is invisible here. And strategies that fail one subject through different routes in turn start each other's run again, since a failure in another domain starts a new one, so they don't decide it. Split-stitch is one atomic retry unit: a transient failure inside it re-runs the whole rung later, paying again for the pieces it had settled, within its per-attempt call caps. Its window cap (`compressionSplitMaxCallsPer10Min`) is a local throttle, not an answer: a rung starts only when the window has room for a whole attempt. Otherwise its chunk waits, with nothing recorded and nothing counted, while the tick goes on to the work behind it, and the first tick after the window has room takes it up (the strategy schedules no tick of its own). A piece's call that the caller's provider admission defers takes no place in the window. An attempt that alone needs more calls than the window allows still ends the rung, as the per-attempt cap does.
- Compression is paced across ticks after a transient failure, in L1 or a merge. No compression call is made and no further queue item is consumed until both the provider's stated `retryAfterMs` and a local backoff of 30 s doubling to 10 minutes have passed. A chunk a failing tick was trying goes behind the chunks that haven't failed, so they run once the pause ends, and a failing merge goes behind the other queued merges. The order lives in the process and every rebuild applies it, so an inbound message doesn't put a failed chunk back at the head, and a failed chunk gives its turn to a queued merge that hasn't failed since, so a lone failing chunk doesn't keep merges, and the landings they bring, from running. A chunk is put back by its source identity, only while it is still uncompressed; a rebuild during the call re-decides its eligibility, and work that rebuild withdrew stays withdrawn. After a deferral by the caller's provider admission or an abort, which say nothing, the chunk keeps its place: at the head, or among the failed if it failed before. A merge spanning a chunk that was put back waits for it: a run of summaries before a hole that a pending retry will fill isn't consolidated early as a stranded interior run (a run whose chunk a rebuild re-cut holds nothing).
  - A subject that fails while nothing else lands in its domain has nothing to compare against, so it is counted no further than its first failure and backs off indefinitely. After an hour of failures, and the waits they caused, with nothing landing, the lane alerts once per stall: a warning, a `compression:lane-stalled` log event, and a structured `compressionStall` in `checkReadiness()` (since when, what failed latest, its failure class), shown while an attempt is in flight too, until a compression lands or an idle hour ends the stretch. Only a failure starts the clock: a hold by the caller's provider admission with no failure before it starts none. Within a stretch of failures, a pause a failure installed keeps the lane waiting, not idle, while it binds, even with no tick to see it: an explicit hold until it is lifted, and a backoff or a stated wait until it ends, or until an answer, a release or a change of route ends it sooner. The pauses are those of every strategy of the process on the store and namespace, whatever their branch, since a pause binds its route on every branch; a strategy stops counting when its manager closes or replaces it (`ContextStrategy.detach()`). An attempt that runs long isn't idle either. A tick during any wait or hold shows the stall, and a hold by the caller's admission is seen only through such ticks. An hour in which the lane was neither failing, nor running an attempt, nor waiting on a pause (nothing pending, or the process down) is idle: the stretch ends at the next attempt or paused tick, with no landing, and the next failure starts the clock again. The clock and the alert are kept in branch-scoped state (`autobio:compression-stall`), shared by the strategies on the store and namespace. So a restart keeps them and alerts no second time; a restart's pauses are gone, so after a down time of an hour or more, which is idle, the next attempt or paused tick ends the old stretch; another branch shows its own; and a landing by any of those strategies ends the stall, through any membrane or model, so a stuck route's stall can hide behind another route's landings. If the provider is answering, the request may be failing on its own: the stall shows until anything lands; once other work lands on its own route its failures count, and the eighth counted one decides it, unless someone fixes the cause first.
  - A stated wait is a non-negative `retryAfterMs` (NaN, or any value that isn't a non-negative number, states none) and is never shortened. One that cannot be held as an instant (Infinity, or past the last instant a Date can hold) is an explicit hold until a restart, a model change, or a release.
  - Any provider answer to one of the lane's calls resets its route's pause, the pause of the membrane instance and model it went through, a merge's answer included (a refusal is an answer), whatever the branch: the pause is the route's. Another route's answer leaves it.
  - The pause is in process and scoped to the membrane instance and to the model the failed call was dispatched with: another model ignores and clears it. A restart-stable provider deadline belongs to the caller's provider admission. Discarded or stale-branch work installs nothing. `checkReadiness()` names a pause only while it binds the configured model.
  - `releaseCompressionPause(model?)` releases the provider's stated wait (agent-framework calls it when an operator releases a provider wait). The lane's own backoff stays, so the lane resumes once that has passed too, and the failure count stays (a release is not an answer from the provider).
  - A refusal by the caller's provider admission (`providerAdmission: 'deferred'`: agent-framework holding a model, no call made) counts no failure and adds no backoff; any genuine backoff already in force is left as it was. The admission stays the authority: its answer is cached until the deadline it gave or for at most 30 s (`COMPRESSION_ADMISSION_RECHECK_MS`), whichever is sooner, and the lane then asks it again. Asking makes no provider call. The cached answer expires within 30 s, and the next otherwise-eligible tick asks the admission again (a genuine backoff, or the maintenance cadence, can postpone that tick), so a condition that clears with no release reaching this instance (a recovered journal, a release made in another process) is observed without one; `releaseCompressionPause` still resumes at once. A deferral never shortens a wait a provider stated after a real call.
  - One warning per pause, a `compression:lane-paused` log event, and `checkReadiness()` names the pause while it lasts. A hold by the caller's provider admission warns, and logs `compression:lane-held-by-admission`, once per hold and again when its deadline changes, though the lane asks the admission again every 30 s at most.
- The merge lane routes only request-attributable rejections through its bounded attempt policy. A non-retryable failure that says nothing about the request (an expired key, a spent quota) now pauses the lane instead of spending attempts toward quarantine. Any other failure counts under the same evidence rule as L1's: the 8th failure in a run (the first, and seven more each after a landing in its domain) is a bounded-policy attempt, which pauses nothing (as in L1, the deciding failure is the request's own), so the policy's smaller retry is asked at the next tick. A rejection is recognized by its type whatever value is thrown, and any answer ends the entry's run. A merge whose answer a compression hold placed during the call keeps out is no failure: it stays queued for the release, pausing nothing and counting nothing. A queued merge that is skipped with no call (a source missing or already parented, or a crossed merge refused) is dropped from the queue as before, but it is neither an answer that ends the pause nor a landing. For subclasses: the protected `executeMerge` now resolves `true` when it wrote the merged parent and `false` when it skipped, and an override must resolve `true` for a merge it wrote. The persisted merge entry carries the run in place of its server-error streak, so the merge lane no longer counts 12 consecutive server errors in an outage.
- Compression failure logs (`Failed to compress chunk`, `Failed to merge summaries`, the speculative-drain error) print a bounded string instead of the error object, and the L1 and merge `CONTEXT_MANAGER_COMPRESSION_LOG` records bound their `error` field to 2,000 characters. A provider error's `rawRequest` is the whole request that failed, and printing the object wrote it out in full.
