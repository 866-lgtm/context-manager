- Compression prompts replace serialized `data:<mime>;base64,…` URLs whose payload has at least 4096 characters with a marker naming the media type and the payload's length. Imported tool output can carry a whole image as text, which the image-byte cap can't see and the tokenizer prices as prose, so one image could cost hundreds of thousands of tokens. Text blocks and tool results (string or block array) are covered, with MIME parameters before `;base64`, JSON-escaped slashes and any letter case; line-wrapped and bare base64 aren't. Chronicle content is never changed.
- Every compression request is prepared the same way before its admission, hash and dispatch: canonical and source-only L1 requests, recall-curve fallbacks, split parts, mode rewrites, merges and transition summaries. A request gets its own message wrappers and copies of what preparation changes, so preparing one never edits a stored message or another request. Recall-curve fallbacks are built from the unprepared answers and prepared on their own, so a parent answer carrying a data URL keeps its fallback, and the children's answers are prepared too.
- A block that preparation edits gives up its replay form (`rawXml`, `rawItem`), and so does every block in its message that shares one with it, so the old payload can't be replayed in place of the marker. Blocks that share no replay form with an edited block keep their identities and replay forms.
- The compression image cap's log line names the request it was prepared for, and a line of its own counts the data URLs replaced.
